MTC Strategic Supply Chain Improvement Plan

An integrated roadmap for demand planning, inventory, logistics, hospital partnerships, supplier strategy, and make-or-buy decisions.

Executive Summary

MTC should shift from a high-inventory, fragmented logistics model toward a demand-driven and more visible supply chain. The recommendations below focus on improving forecasting, reducing unnecessary inventory touchpoints, controlling field stock, strengthening hospital partnerships, and improving the outsourced sterilization process while preserving service reliability.

Demand-driven planningUse seasonality and recent shipment trends instead of a relatively flat production plan.
Lower inventory exposureReduce duplicate stock across warehouses, branches, loaner locations, and sales representatives.
Strategic customer valueCompete on reliability, information, utilization improvement, and clinical support—not unit price alone.

1. Current Product Flow and Logistics Issues

Manufacturing→ Off-site Sterilization→ Central Warehouse→ Distributor / 3PL→ Branch / Loaner Office→ Hospital

An additional urgent channel uses sales-representative trunk stock to serve hospitals directly. While responsive, this structure creates multiple inventory touchpoints, longer lead times, and limited visibility over stock held outside the central network.

  • Reduce unnecessary handling and duplicate inventory locations.
  • Use barcode or RFID visibility across central, regional, hospital, and field inventory.
  • Segment products by urgency, demand predictability, and value instead of using one logistics model for all products.

2. Forecasting and Production Planning

MTC's demand pattern is seasonal, so production should not remain relatively constant across the year. A seasonal forecast with a modest trend adjustment better aligns output with actual shipment behavior.

  • Increase production ahead of historically strong demand months.
  • Reduce production sharply during predictable low-demand periods instead of building unnecessary inventory.
  • Connect the forecast to sterilization capacity, transportation planning, and safety-stock decisions.
  • Review forecast error monthly and update the plan as new actual demand becomes available.

3. Inventory and Trunk Stock Policy

MTC should not eliminate sales-representative trunk stock completely. Instead, trunk stock should be treated as a controlled emergency inventory buffer.

Keep in trunk stock

High-priority products with urgent demand, high service requirements, and frequent usage.

Move out of trunk stock

Slow-moving, expensive, or highly duplicative products that can be held at controlled regional locations.

Control mechanism

Set minimum and maximum levels, track items electronically, and return or reallocate stock that has not moved within a defined period.

4. Positioning MTC as a Strategic Hospital Supplier

MTC should become part of the hospital's operating system rather than function only as a medical product vendor. Strategic supplier behavior should include shared planning, measurable service commitments, and joint improvement.

  • Establish service-level agreements covering availability, delivery speed, emergency support, and inventory accuracy.
  • Share demand and utilization information to identify shortages, slow-moving stock, and unnecessary duplication.
  • Participate in joint planning and value-analysis discussions with procurement and clinical teams.
  • Support product standardization, clinical education, and process-improvement initiatives.

Top three partnership activities: joint forecasting and inventory planning; product standardization and utilization management; vendor-managed or consignment inventory.

5. External Sterilization: Make-or-Buy Recommendation

MTC should keep sterilization outsourced in the near term, but manage the relationship more strategically.

Reasons to outsource

  • Specialized regulatory and technical expertise
  • Avoid major fixed investment
  • Capacity flexibility and risk sharing

Reasons to internalize

  • Shorter lead time
  • Greater schedule and quality control
  • Better integration with manufacturing

The recommended compromise is to retain a qualified external specialist while tightening service-level agreements, tracking lead-time and quality performance, coordinating transportation more closely, and evaluating a provider located closer to the manufacturing site.

6. Implementation Roadmap

0–3 monthsImprove inventory visibility, define trunk-stock rules, establish logistics KPIs, and begin forecast-error reviews.
3–9 monthsSegment inventory, consolidate selected logistics points, launch hospital collaboration pilots, and renegotiate sterilization SLAs.
9–18 monthsExpand VMI/consignment programs, optimize the regional network, and reassess sterilization location and total cost.

Key Performance Indicators

Service level Inventory turns Forecast error Stockouts Expedited freight Sterilization lead time Field inventory aging Total logistics cost

The overall objective is to lower total supply chain cost while maintaining or improving product availability for hospitals and patients.
